2024 / 10 / 9 Joseph Hoon NUS
U.S. State-Level Economic Activity Since the Civil War
Macro Econ History
We introduce a novel state-level dataset for the United States that covers 63 macroeconomic time series since 1863 and use it to estimate an annual index of state-level economic activity over a long term. We document several new stylized facts about U.S. state-level business cycles which allow us to identify determinants of aggregate fluctuations.
2024 / 10 / 23 Jingyuan Guo NUS
State Capacity Building and Economic Growth: Evidence from Deng Xiaoping's Bloodless Coup D'état
Development Political Econ
This paper explores how change in state capacity facilitates economic growth in an authoritarian system. Using a continuous treatment DiD strategy, it reveals that a one percent increase in officials' replacement intensity results in a 1.3 percent increase in GDP in post-Mao China, with effects driven by increases in human capital, governance capability, market-friendly industrialization, and pluralistic political institutions.
2024 / 11 / 6 Jingman Cao NUS
Generative AI and Labor Market Inequality: Evidence from a Global Freelance Market
Labor Platform / Digital
This paper examines the impact of ChatGPT on freelancers with varying levels of English proficiency in a global online labor market. Since ChatGPT's introduction, the earnings and job count gap between native and non-native English speakers has narrowed, with non-native speakers securing more complex and higher-paying jobs with enhanced productivity.
2024 / 12 / 4 Honghao Zheng KU Leuven
Death by Market Power: The Production-Safety Tradeoff in the Coal Mining Industry
IO Environmental Labor
This paper examines the effects of downstream buyer power from coal-fired power plants on the upstream coal mining industry in China. Using a shift-share IV, the study finds that buyer power exposure leads to higher provincial death rates by prompting mines to shift toward less capital-intensive, less safe technologies. Back-of-the-envelope calculations suggest the decline in buyer power explains 53% of the improvement in coal mining death rates.

2025 / 2 / 28 Jingman Cao NUS
When AI Writes Alike: Implications for Job Matching Efficiency
Labor Platform / Digital
Studying Upwork's Job Post Generator, this paper finds that AI-driven content homogenization increases average pairwise cosine similarity of job postings by 18%. Clients who heavily rely on unmodified AI-generated content experience longer matching times, while those who actively edit AI-generated postings enjoy better matching efficiency, lower costs, and higher satisfaction.
2025 / 3 / 14 Xinbo Wang NUS
The Demand for Counterfeits
IO Consumer Behavior
Using 11 million orders from 2 million U.S. consumers on a cross-border e-commerce platform, this paper documents that socioeconomic status has a non-monotonic relationship with counterfeit purchasing, local culture (trust, social capital) significantly affects demand, and consumer-generated quality information can increase counterfeit sales.

2025 / 9 / 18 Gaoyang Cai Northwestern University
Information Disclosure and Efficiency in Dynamic Negotiations: An Experimental and Structural Analysis
IO Market Design Platform / Digital
Using a large-scale panel experiment on an on-demand delivery platform, this paper shows that full price disclosure increases rejection rates and induces strategic price discrimination. A dynamic negotiation model reveals that partial disclosure policies achieve Pareto improvements in welfare by mitigating signaling externalities, with optimized thresholds increasing social welfare by 23% relative to no-disclosure and 14.1% relative to full-disclosure benchmarks.
2025 / 9 / 25 Orlando Betancourt NUS
Awards and Diversity: Evidence from Hollywood
Labor Political Econ
Exploiting the timing of Academy Award nominations since 1927, this paper finds that while nominations increase women's career activity, the boost is significantly larger for men. Women also face gaps in salary growth and future nomination likelihood, with network effects and strategic career choices driving much of the divergence.
2025 / 10 / 2 Zhuoru Ma NTU
Beyond Diagnosis: The Hidden Health Burden of Long-term PM2.5 Exposure
Health Environmental
Combining CHARLS data with satellite-based PM2.5 estimates and wind-direction IVs, this paper finds that air pollution significantly increases biomarker-identified respiratory disease risk (0.33 pp per 1% increase in PM2.5), while self-reported diagnoses show no corresponding effect — revealing systematic underdiagnosis particularly among middle-aged males and those in areas with limited healthcare resources.
2025 / 10 / 16 Shuting Zhang NUS
Steady Home amid Turbulence: Equity Shocks and Reallocation into Housing
Urban / Real Estate Finance
Using China's 2014–2015 stock market episode and granular housing transactions, this paper shows that sharp equity swings reallocate demand into property markets. Communities near listed firms show 1.25% larger price increases during the run-up and 2.15% larger increases after the crash, driven primarily by transactions involving employees of the listed firm.
2025 / 10 / 23 Zhu Jianfei HKU
From Law to Livelihood: How Legal Development Shapes Informal Finance and Household Behavior
Finance Development Law & Econ
Leveraging a Chinese reform strengthening protections for digital P2P lenders, this paper uses a DiD framework on FinTech transaction data to show that stronger legal enforcement expanded informal credit access, raised repayment rates, and induced substitution from formal to informal financing — with borrowers increasing discretionary spending on human capital such as vocational training.
2025 / 10 / 30 Jiani Wu NTU
How Investor Information Linkages Affect Trading Aggressiveness, Price Informativeness, and Post-Earnings-Announcement Drift
Finance Experimental
This dynamic asset market experiment finds that trading aggressiveness first increases then declines as information linkages get denser, while price informativeness continuously improves. Denser pre-announcement information linkages reduce the magnitude of post-earnings-announcement drift, confirming that social connectedness is an important determinant of market efficiency.

2026 / 5 / 12 En Qi Teo NUS / TSE
Submissions Related to Editors' Research Are More Likely to Succeed: Evidence from Economics Journals
IO Econ of Science
Examining four leading economics journals, this paper finds that editors are less likely to desk reject manuscripts related to their own research interests. Structural estimates indicate the mechanism is strategic submission by authors rather than editorial favoritism, with more productive scholars disproportionately likely to submit related manuscripts.
