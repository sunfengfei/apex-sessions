2026 / 05 / 05 Yuan Tian Tsinghua University
The Evaluation of the Impact of Generative Artificial Intelligence on Innovation in Biopharmaceutical Enterprises
Innovation & Growth
This study investigates whether generative artificial intelligence (AI) has materially improved firm-level innovation in the biopharmaceutical industry. Using Chinese A-share listed biopharmaceutical manufacturing firms from 2017 to 2024, we treat the recent wave of generative AI and AI for Science, represented by AlphaFold2 and ChatGPT, as an exogenous technological shock and employ a difference-in-differences design to identify its causal effect on corporate innovation output. Firms ranked in the top 10% of industry AI penetration in 2021 are defined as the treatment group, while the remaining firms serve as the control group. Innovation output is measured by the logarithm of one plus the number of invention patent applications. The results show that generative AI significantly increases innovation output among firms with stronger pre-existing AI capabilities. After controlling for firm fixed effects, year fixed effects, and firm-level characteristics, the interaction term remains significantly positive, suggesting that the post-shock innovation performance of high-AI-exposure firms improves substantially relative to that of other firms. Further analyses indicate that generative AI not only raises patent quantity but also enhances R&D efficiency by improving the conversion of R&D inputs into innovation outputs, thereby providing evidence against the “strategic innovation” explanation. In addition, the effect remains robust after excluding firms located in first-tier cities, implying that generative AI may exhibit technological spillover and inclusive effects across regions.

Does Overseas Education Contribute to the State Capacity?
Development Public & Political Economy
This paper examines whether overseas education can contribute to state capacity in developing countries. Using the expansion of China–Africa educational cooperation under FOCAC, we apply a continuous difference-in-differences design based on countries’ predetermined exposure to Chinese higher education. The results show that FOCAC significantly increased African student inflows to China, but improvements in state capacity are concentrated in countries with lower ethnic fractionalization. In these countries, greater exposure is associated with better bureaucratic quality, higher tax revenue, and evidence of structural transformation. The findings suggest that overseas education can contribute to state building, but its effects depend on domestic institutional conditions.
2026 / 04 / 14 Mengru Wang NUS
Misaligned Boundaries: Border Manipulation in Qing Prefectures
Economic History Public & Political Economy
The strategic manipulation of China's administrative boundaries has long attracted scholarly attention, yet existing work has focused exclusively on the provincial level. This paper examines the existence of border manipulation in Qing China's prefectures. I construct 236 hypothetical prefectures based on geographical attributes, measure their misalignment from actual prefectural borders, and compare the results with provincial-level misalignment. The analysis reveals no evidence of systematic manipulation of prefectural borders, as misalignment is largely uniform across the empire with little spatial correlation. Although deliberate misalignment occurs in certain provinces, this manipulation does not extend to their subordinate prefectures. The results also suggest that provincial manipulation was achieved by reallocating intact prefectures between neighboring provinces. These findings point to a key tradeoff in administrative boundary design between political stability and administrative efficiency.

2025 / 10 / 30 Jiani Wu NTU
How Investor Information Linkages Affect Trading Aggressiveness, Price Informativeness, and Post-Earnings-Announcement Drift
Behavioral & Experiment
We design a dynamic asset market experiment with exogenous information linkages among investors within a public earnings announcement framework. Confirming recent theoretical predictions, we find that trading aggressiveness and volume in the pre-announcement period first increase and then decline while price informativeness continues to improve as information linkages get denser. Information sharing improves traders' inference of the fundamental value and enhances the quality of pre-announcement information among individual investors. This results in a weaker response to the subsequent public earnings announcement, with the magnitude of the post-earnings-announcement drift declining in the density of the pre-announcement information linkages. We demonstrate that social connectedness is an important determinant of market efficiency.
2025 / 10 / 23 Zhu Jianfei HKU
From Law to Livelihood: How Legal Development Shapes Informal Finance and Household Behavior
Development Public & Political Economy
This study examines how legal institutional development shapes informal finance by leveraging a Chinese reform that strengthened protections for digital peer-to-peer lenders. Using a Difference-in-Differences (DiD) framework and transaction-level data from a leading Chinese FinTech platform, I quantify post-reform changes in lending and borrowing within social networks. Strengthened legal enforcement expanded access to informal credit—especially among socially distant peers—and accelerated the formalization of digital transaction records. For borrowers, the reform increased repayment rates in informal markets, induced substitution from formal to informal financing, and raised discretionary spending, particularly on human capital such as vocational training and professional tools. These findings highlight how legal institutions both regulate and legitimize informal finance, with implications for financial inclusion and household welfare.
2025 / 10 / 16 Shuting Zhang NUS
Steady Home amid Turbulence: Equity Shocks and Reallocation into Housing
Transport & Urban
Household wealth is concentrated in housing and equities. We test whether sharp equity swings reallocate demand into property market. Using China's 2014 to 2015 stock market episode, we combine granular and high-frequency housing transactions with equity data and estimate a difference-in-differences design that contrasts communities 0–1 km with those 1–4 km from the same listed firm within month. During the run-up, the near ring records a 1.25% larger price increase relative to the farther ring, and after the crash the near-firm premium is 2.15%. To speak to the mechanisms, residents living close to listed firms are more likely to be employees and better informed about equity conditions, and triple-difference estimates that interact proximity with realized equity movements support this interpretation: when realized volatility is higher, the near-firm differential compresses and can reverse, with a 3.12% decline in the run-up and a 2.85% depreciation after the crash for high-volatility firms. Heterogeneity by buyer and seller links to the firm indicates that transactions involving residents employed by the listed firm within 1 km account for much of the response. Volume effects are insignificantly small, consistent with price revaluation on the intensive margin. Results are robust to alternative specifications and stricter controls and are consistent with local asset reallocation.
2025 / 10 / 2 Zhuoru Ma NTU
Beyond Diagnosis: The Hidden Health Burden of Long-term PM2.5 Exposure
Health Environmental & Resource
This study investigates the hidden health burden of long-term air pollution exposure by comparing self-reported clinical diagnoses with biomarker-identified indicators of respiratory disease. Self-reported diagnoses, commonly used in economic analyses, may underestimate actual disease burden due to underdiagnosis and differential access to healthcare. We examine whether objective biomarkers reveal stronger pollution-health associations than conventional survey-based measures. We combine nationally representative data from the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Survey (CHARLS) with high-resolution satellite-based PM2.5 estimates. To address endogeneity in pollution exposure, we construct instrumental variables based on share of wind direction days interacted with geographic clusters, leveraging quasi-random variation in atmospheric transport patterns. We find that air pollution significantly increases biomarker-identified risks of respiratory disease, by 0.33 percentage points per 1% increase in PM2.5, while no corresponding effect is observed in self-reported diagnoses. Similar patterns of underdiagnosis are found for cardiovascular, kidney and metabolic conditions. Underdiagnosis is more prominent among middle-aged individuals, males, those exposed to secondhand smoke, those with better childhood health, and those living in areas with limited healthcare resources. Our findings suggest that reliance on self-reported diagnoses may systematically understate the true health impacts of air pollution.
2025 / 9 / 25 Orlando Betancourt NUS
Awards and Diversity: Evidence from Hollywood
Labor & Education Public & Political Economy
I examine whether the recognition associated with major awards has different career effects depending on minority status. Exploiting the timing of Academy Award nominations since 1927, I focus on women as the underrepresented group. I find that while a nomination increases women's subsequent career activity -as measured by the number of projects and lead roles-, this increase is significantly larger for men. Women also face gaps in salary growth and in the likelihood of future nominations. Exploring mechanisms, I show that men expand collaborations with other nominees and pursue more "Oscar-bait" projects, suggesting that network effects and strategic career choices might drive the gaps. I rule out childbearing and differences in project quality as alternative explanations.
2025 / 9 / 18 Gaoyang Cai Northwestern University
Information Disclosure and Efficiency in Dynamic Negotiations: An Experimental and Structural Analysis
Industrial Organization Innovation & Growth
This paper examines the causal effects of information disclosure policies on dynamic price negotiations. While information disclosure facilitates informed decision-making by enabling users to reveal private information, it also induces strategic signaling and price discrimination that can undermine market efficiency. I use a large-scale panel experiment on an on-demand delivery platform in which drivers offer delivery prices to shippers (customers). The platform randomly assigns cities either to a treatment group—where drivers observe the lowest prices previously rejected by shippers (full disclosure)—or to a control group, where no such information is disclosed (no disclosure). I find that full disclosure significantly increases shippers' rejection rates, holding offer prices fixed. In response, drivers strategically lower their offer prices and price-discriminate based on shippers' rejection records. Full disclosure increases the order transaction probability but lengthens transaction duration. To quantify equilibrium and welfare impacts of information disclosure policies, I develop a dynamic negotiation model, estimate its primitives on treatment data, and validate the model on holdout control data. I find that full disclosure benefits low-value shippers—who signal low valuations through strategic rejections, obtaining lower prices despite longer waits—while harming high-value shippers through driver price discrimination. Counterfactual analyses show that partial disclosure policies—disclosing only the lowest shipper-rejected prices below a threshold—achieve Pareto improvements in user welfare and platform revenue by mitigating negative signaling externalities, reducing driver markups, and enhancing market efficiency. Comparative statics show that the disclosure thresholds that maximize driver profits, platform revenue, and social welfare rise as shippers become less patient, as exit rates increase, or as drivers arrive less often. Targeting market primitives to optimize market-specific disclosure thresholds increases social welfare by 23.0% and 14.1% relative to the no disclosure and full disclosure benchmarks, respectively.

The Demand for Counterfeits
Industrial Organization Behavioral & Experiment
Using 11 million orders from 2 million U.S. consumers on a major cross-border e-commerce platform, we document three main findings on counterfeit demand. First, socioeconomic status has a non-monotonic relationship with purchasing behavior: both low- and high-income consumers purchase more fakes than those in the middle. Second, local culture exerts a significant effect: communities characterized by lower trust buy more counterfeits, while communities with stronger social capital buy less counterfeits. Third, market design matters: consumer-generated product-quality information can increase counterfeit sales. We explore heterogeneous effects for each finding and discuss their policy implications.
2025 / 2 / 28 Jingman Cao NUS
When AI Writes Alike: Implications for Job Matching Efficiency
Labor & Education Innovation & Growth
Online platforms are introducing various kinds of generative AI tools after the emerging of ChatGPT. Although these tools could improve work productivity, they could also introduce issues of writing content convergence, which might result in increased screening difficulty and lower matching efficiency. In this paper, we study Job Post Generator, a generative AI tool launched by a freelance platform, Upwork. After its introduction, the average pairwise cosine similarity of job postings increased by 18% from 2023 March to 2024 September. We find that clients who use the Job Post Generator match with freelancers more quickly on average. However, those who rely heavily on AI-generated content with minimal modifications experience longer matching times. In contrast, clients who actively edit and customize AI-generated job postings experience significant improvements in matching efficiency, benefit from lower payment costs, and report higher satisfaction with freelancers' performance. We provide evidence that the homogenization of job postings due to AI-driven writing can negatively impact online job matching efficiency. Our findings suggest that online platforms offering generative AI tools should encourage users to modify and personalize AI-generated content to achieve better outcomes.

Death by Market Power: The Production-Safety Tradeoff in the Coal Mining Industry
Industrial Organization Environmental & Resource Labor & Education
I examine the effects of downstream buyer power from coal-fired power plants on the organization of production in the upstream coal mining industry in China. I estimate a structural model of coal mines featuring (i) joint production of coal and worker safety and (ii) endogenous safety choices and factor-augmenting productivity. To identify the causal effects of buyer power on coal mining outcomes, I employ a shift-share instrumental variable, leveraging exogenous variations stemming from a restructuring of the electricity sector. I find an unintended but life-and-death consequence associated with market power—buyer power exposure leads to higher provincial death rates, also corroborated by lower safety-coal output ratios at the mine level. The underlying mechanism is that exposure to buyer power prompts coal mines to shift toward less capital-intensive, more traditional, and less safe mining technologies, leading to higher death rates. Finally, back-of-the-envelope calculations suggest that the decline in buyer power due to the electricity sector restructuring explains 53% of the improvement in coal mining death rates. The findings provide a new perspective on understanding the shared experience of high mining death rates in developed and developing countries over different historical periods.
2024 / 11 / 6 Jingman Cao NUS
Generative AI and Labor Market Inequality: Evidence from a Global Freelance Market
Labor & Education Innovation & Growth
Generative AI technologies have the potential to either exacerbate or alleviate labor market inequalities. This paper examines the impact of ChatGPT on freelancers with varying levels of English proficiency in a global online labor market. We find that, since ChatGPT's introduction, the earnings and job count gap between native and non-native English speakers have narrowed. Our analysis reveals that non-native speakers have become more competitive, securing more complex and higher-paying jobs with enhanced productivity, and experiencing lower job displacement. These findings suggest that generative AI can play a meaningful role in reducing disparities in employment opportunities in the global labor market.

U.S. State-Level Economic Activity Since the Civil War
Economic History
We introduce a novel state-level dataset for the United States that covers 63 macroeconomic time series since 1863 and use it to estimate an annual index of state-level economic activity over a long term. We document several new stylized facts about U.S. state-level business cycles which allow us to identify determinants of aggregate fluctuations.
